seo has a reputation problem because an industry got built on making it sound complicated. here's the honest version.

## what google actually wants

google wants to answer its user's question with the best page. that's the entire game. every "ranking factor" is just google asking: is this page fast? is it clear? does it say what the searcher needs? do other sites treat it like it's real?

## what your site needs (and gets, standard)

**speed.** if your page takes five seconds to load, google shows someone else's. every site i ship scores fast on google's own test — not because of tricks, because it's built right.

**structure.** google reads your site like a document. clear titles, clean headings, a sitemap that lists every page, structured data that says "this is a restaurant, here are the hours." machines shouldn't have to guess.

**real words.** a page that says "welcome to our website" ranks for nothing. a page that says "wood-fired pizza in cookeville, open till 11" ranks for exactly that. i write the words with you.

## the new part nobody's doing yet: ai visibility

when someone asks chatgpt "who builds fences in baton rouge," the models pull from the same structured data google reads — plus how clearly your pages state what you do and where. sites built with clean structure get named. sites built as a pile of pretty pictures don't. every site i ship includes this. most agencies haven't caught up yet.

## what you don't need

a $500/month "seo retainer" for a five-page local business site. after launch, seo is mostly: keep the information true, add a page when you add a service, collect reviews. i show you how to do all three in the admin.
