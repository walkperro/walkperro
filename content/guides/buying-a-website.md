most people buying their first website have no idea what they're actually buying. that's not your fault — nobody explains it. here's the whole thing, no jargon.

## the week, day by day

**day 1 — the call.** you tell me about the business. what it does, who calls you, what you want more of. i ask annoying questions on purpose. by the end we both know exactly what the site has to do.

**days 2–3 — the build.** i build the real thing. not a mockup, not a wireframe — the actual site, on a real link you can open on your phone.

**day 4 — your pass.** you click through it and tell me what's wrong. the photo you hate, the wording that isn't you, the button that should be bigger. i fix it same-day.

**day 5 — live.** your domain points at it. google gets told it exists. you get the keys — a simple admin page where you can change things yourself.

## what you own when it's done

everything. the domain is registered to you. the site code is yours. if we part ways tomorrow, you keep it all. no hostage situations.

## what it costs after

hosting for a site like this is effectively free to cheap — i set you up on infrastructure that doesn't charge until you're big enough to celebrate it. the domain runs about $12 a year. that's it. no monthly "maintenance fee" for a site that just sits there working.

## the one thing i'll push back on

if you ask for ten pages, i'll talk you down to five. more pages isn't more professional — it's more places for a customer to get lost. the site's job is to make the phone ring. everything that doesn't serve that gets cut.
